# NexoChess v1.4

Release date: 24 August 2026.

NexoChess v1.4 closes the current product-development cycle by connecting game review, historical diagnosis and targeted training much more tightly. The release also adds a dedicated Statistics center, an Endgame Laboratory and a contextual tutorial, while continuing the performance and UX work from v1.3.

## Analysis: from review to conclusions

- Every analysed game can now open a **Conclusions** view alongside the normal move-by-move Review.
- Conclusions are generated deterministically from the existing Stockfish evaluation, expected-points loss, classifications, game phase and tactical detectors. No generative-AI service is required.
- The view identifies a decisive moment, the most important error/pattern and a concrete training recommendation.
- Each conclusion can jump directly back to the exact reviewed position.
- Tactical explanation buttons follow the engine line more consistently and coach text avoids misleading invented continuations.
- Brilliant-move classification is stricter: ordinary pawn sacrifices are not Brilliant, while genuine sound piece sacrifices and exceptional drawing resources remain eligible.
- Sharing analysed games is kept beside the board rather than consuming permanent top-navigation space.

## Statistics and historical diagnosis

A new first-class **Statistics** section has been added to the main navigation. The previous Progress view was removed from Archive and expanded substantially here.

Available samples include the last 10, 20, 50 or 100 personal games, the last 7, 30 or 90 days, the last year and the complete available history.

Statistics can show, when the underlying data exists:

- average accuracy and accuracy evolution;
- wins, draws, losses and score percentage;
- estimated performance;
- White versus Black accuracy, score and performance;
- mistakes, misses and blunders, including rates per 100 player moves;
- error distribution and accuracy by opening, middlegame and endgame;
- move-quality distribution;
- best and lowest-accuracy games;
- opening frequency, score, accuracy, performance and W-D-L;
- current puzzle rating, rated attempts, accuracy and streaks;
- lesson completion and Stockfish Duel activity.

Detailed archived games are loaded in batches instead of all at once so a large Archive does not block the interface.

### Brilliant-move gallery

Statistics also includes a personal Brilliant gallery. Every stored `!!` in the selected sample is represented by a compact board card without analysis arrows. Opening a card returns to the archived game, enters Review and selects the exact ply that produced the Brilliant move.

## Archive and automatic training plan

Archive remains the long-term game store and now includes an automatic **Training plan**.

- Recent analysed games are inspected for recurring serious errors.
- Weaknesses can be inferred from game phase, conservative tactical motifs, opening results, colour splits and selected endgame patterns.
- The plan converts those signals into short, concrete tasks and routes the player to the relevant NexoChess training area.
- The detector intentionally avoids claiming a recurring weakness from a single isolated mistake.

Together with per-game Conclusions, this creates a continuous loop: analyse a game, understand what mattered, detect recurrence across games and train the relevant skill.

## Endgame Laboratory

Lessons now contains a separate **Endgame Laboratory** entry between the Lessons heading and the lesson-path container.

The initial laboratory contains **72 playable positions** across fundamental, intermediate and advanced levels, covering 18 themes such as:

- basic mates;
- king and pawn endings;
- opposition and key squares;
- pawn races and rook-pawn exceptions;
- Lucena and Philidor;
- rook activity, cutting off the king and side checks;
- minor-piece endings;
- triangulation and zugzwang;
- fortresses;
- queen-versus-pawn and queen-versus-rook technique.

For eligible positions with up to seven pieces, the laboratory uses tablebase information to judge the theoretical W/D/L result exactly. It can therefore detect transitions such as win → draw or draw → loss rather than relying on an approximate engine score.

The laboratory also supports hints, retry after losing the theoretical result, custom FEN loading and persistent mastery by theme.

To keep Lessons fast, the laboratory itself is lazy-loaded and its fundamental, intermediate and advanced position packs are separate chunks. Opening Lessons does not download the full endgame catalogue.

## Repertoire redesign

Repertoire received a substantial study-tree redesign aimed at large opening courses.

- compact decision-tree layout;
- progressive branch expansion instead of rendering huge trees at once;
- move popularity and clearer branch hierarchy;
- transposition handling;
- position and line search;
- fullscreen study mode;
- semantic zoom and more compact navigation for very large repertoires.

The goal is to keep opening families with hundreds of variations readable without kilometres of connecting lines or unnecessary horizontal scrolling.

## Puzzle training

Puzzles is now centred on deliberate practice rather than duplicating analysed-game review.

- cleaner setup before a training session;
- thematic and difficulty filtering;
- positions derived from analysed mistakes focus on mistakes, misses and blunders rather than ordinary inaccuracies;
- training data feeds the broader Statistics and training-plan experience where reliable stored history exists.

The open catalogue remains separate from the main application bundle, so the browser does not download millions of positions at startup.

## Guided NexoChess tutorial

A new optional tutorial explains the purpose of the main sections and important controls without forcing the user to navigate into them.

- launched from a fixed `?` button on eligible section menus;
- absent from the public Home page;
- excluded from Repertoire because Repertoire already has its own specialised tutorial;
- shown only on setup/main-menu states for areas such as Puzzles and Duel, with Analysis as the board-present exception;
- highlights the relevant control while keeping the launcher fixed;
- follows page scrolling where necessary;
- supports keyboard navigation, responsive layouts, reduced-motion preferences and all 11 interface languages.

## Performance, accessibility and localisation

v1.4 continues to prefer progressive and lazy loading for heavy features. The Statistics details, Endgame Laboratory packs, tutorial and release messaging are designed not to inflate every initial page load unnecessarily.

New user-facing work is integrated with dark/light appearance, responsive layouts and the 11 supported languages: English, Spanish, French, German, Portuguese, Russian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Hindi, Marathi and Polish.

## Release notice

The v1.4 Home notice is available only through **27 August 2026 at 23:59 CEST**. It is designed to appear at most once per browser/device and, for signed-in users, at most once per account by persisting a release-version marker in the existing account release-note store.

## Search indexing

This release deliberately does not perform the final sitemap/Search Console/indexing overhaul. Personal Statistics remains non-indexable. Search-indexing work is being handled as a separate final operational pass so product changes and search troubleshooting are not mixed together.

## Release workflow

As with previous versions, feature work is integrated into `develop`, validated by CI and CodeQL and tested on staging before any explicit promotion to `master` and production.
